
AI-BASED ACCESSIBILITY AND TRAFFIC-AWARE ROUTE
RECOMMENDATION FOR PUBLIC SPACES USING COMPUTER VISION

1. OBJECTIVE
To experiment with vehicle detection and tracking on a sample
traffic video using computer vision.

2. TECHNOLOGY USED
- Python
- Google Colab
- Ultralytics YOLO11n
- OpenCV
- ByteTrack object tracker

3. VIDEO DETAILS
- Input file: road_trafifc.mp4
- Frames processed: 301
- Resolution: 640 x 360
- Frame rate: approximately 29.97 FPS
- Duration: approximately 10.04 seconds

4. EXPERIMENT SETTINGS
- Confidence threshold: 0.50
- IoU threshold: 0.30 in the improved detection test
- Tracker: ByteTrack

5. PRELIMINARY TRACKING RESULTS
- Cars: 20 tracked IDs
- Buses: 2 tracked IDs
- Trucks: 1 tracked ID
- Motorcycles: 7 tracked IDs
- Total: 29 tracked IDs

These are estimated tracking IDs, not verified counts of
unique physical vehicles.

6. DETECTION EXPERIMENT
In the tested frame, lowering the IoU threshold to 0.30
reduced the bus detections from two boxes to one box.
Across the full video, the maximum number of bus boxes
in one frame was one in the tested configuration.

7. LIMITATIONS
- The same physical bus may receive multiple tracking IDs.
- Vehicle detections may be missed or incorrectly classified.
- Results come from a short sample video, not live citywide traffic.
- Accessibility barriers such as ramps, stairs, and blocked
  sidewalks have not yet been validated by this experiment.
- Traffic levels cannot be reliably assigned from these counts
  alone without a defined classification method.

8. NEXT STEPS
- Validate detections and tracking on additional video frames.
- Define a reliable traffic-level classification method.
- Develop or integrate an accessibility observation workflow.
- Connect the outputs to the backend and route recommendation system.
